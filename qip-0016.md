## Abstract

Coinbase rewards in both the Quai and Qi ledgers are locked before they can be spent. A miner can choose to lock their coinbase rewards for longer to receive a larger reward upon unlock. The mechanism for unlocking Quai and Qi coinbases is different, but here I propose a mechanism that unifies the unlocking process and allows a miner to delegate their reward to a smart contract that manages it for them. This allows for the creation of a "Liquid Mining Token" (LMT) which is a non-fungible token that represents a set of coinbase rewards and an unlock date.

## Motivation

We have decided to lock coinbase rewards as a mechanism to control inflation and to create a bond market whereby a user can choose to lock their Qi or Quai tokens for a predetermined amount of time to receive interest. However, because only miners can create a locked position via their coinbase rewards, the average user who does not own mining hardware would have trouble creating a position themselves, and even most miners wouldn't be able to take on a large locked position without the help of other miners. To properly create a bond market, we need a mechanism to create abstracted forms of these locked coinbase rewards so that they can be bought and sold on a DEX. As demand for bonded assets grows, more miners will decide to lock their positions, further delaying Quai and Qi inflation into the future.

## Specification

### Coinbase Lockups

The following table displays the interest rate given to locked coinbase rewards based on the lockup byte and timeframe. The longer the lockup timeframe, the higher the expected return at maturity.

| Lockup Byte       | Reward                | Timeframe                         
|-------------------|-----------------------|-------------------
| 0                 | No reward             | 7200 blocks (~12 hours)                      
| 1                 | +4.16%                | 30240 blocks (~1.75 days)                   
| 2                 | +10%                  | 60480 blocks (~3.5 days)
| 3                 | +25%                  | 120960 blocks (~7 days)

This QIP will not make an economic argument for or against interest rates on locked rewards, nor will it attempt to determine what the interest rates ought to be. Instead, this QIP will explain how the protocol works from a technical perspective.

When a miner finds a workshare that meets the difficulty threshold, the miner sends it to his node, and the node determines if it's classified as a block, a workshare, or a "sub-share." A block is an object that extends the canonical chain and commits transactions to the state, a workshare commits to a prior block and increases the intrinsic work of the canonical chain, and a sub-share is used only for transaction propagation and is not rewarded nor does it contribute to the intrinsic work of the chain. 

When a block or workshare is found, an ETX is created that holds the miner's reward. The ETX is sent to Prime and then sent back down to Zone, where it is executed in a new block and the reward is locked. Both blocks and workshares are classified as Work Objects and therefore they both have a Data field in the Work Object Header. All miners must place a lockup byte as the first byte in the Data field for the workshare or block to be valid. The lockup byte determines the interest rate and how long the coinbase reward will be locked for. A miner may optionally insert a 20-byte smart contract address immediately after the lockup byte in the Data field (for a total of 21 bytes) if they wish to allow a smart contract to manage their locked coinbase position for them. This address MUST be a smart contract address (must have code deployed to the address) otherwise the coinbase is lost. A miner may also add an additional 20-byte address immediately after the first address (for a total of 41 bytes) if they wish to have a second smart contract manage the assets that the first smart contract holds on their behalf. More on this "delegate" address later.

When a miner finds a block or workshare with a smart contract address in the data field, an ETX is created, and when executed, a new bond position is created in the node database. This bond position collapses and stores all coinbases for that miner, smart contract, and lockup byte combination for a period of CoinbaseEpochBlocks. That is, all coinbases that are found in the CoinbaseEpochBlocks window of time (typically 1 week) are added to this position, and the unlock height for the position remains the unlock height of the first coinbase. This coinbase lockup position may be referred to as a *bond* or *tranche* interchangeably. Each tranche (with combination of miner, smart contract, and lockup byte) is assigned a unique number, called an epoch, which is the global epoch number at the time of the tranche creation. Every "CoinbaseEpochBlocks" blocks, the global epoch number is incremented, effectively sealing off eery miner's previous tranche from future additions. When a tranche is sealed, it can no logner be modified, and therefore can be tokenized by the contract the owns it. 

Note: When a miner decides to give their coinbase to a smart contract, they are entrusting control of their rewards to that contract. It is important that a miner understands the risk of doing so, because from the perspective of the protocol, the miner technically no longer owns the reward - rather, the coinbase is now a bond and the contract controls the bond on behalf of the miner.

### Smart Contracts, NFTs and more

Ideally, just as a miner can create a bond, so too should a user be able to purchase one from a miner. If user demand for bonds increases, more miners would switch to mining bonds, which would further delay inflation into the future. Conversely, if no users demand bonds, miners can mine bonds and hold them to maturity for an increase in their rewards. 

In order to allow users to purchase bonds, the protocol allows for miners to create tranches, which is effectively a bond position with an interest rate and a maturity date. The miner gives control of this position to a smart contract, and a smart contract mints a tokenized form of this position to the miner, likely constructed as a non-fungible token, or NFT (ERC-1155). When a miner receives this non-fungible token, they can then list it on a decentralized exchange, where users can view various bonds and purchase them in bulk.

A **tranche** is keyed by the following fields: smart contract address, miner address, lockup byte, epoch

A **tranche** has the following information stored in it: balance, unlock height, number of elements, delegate address

To faciliate this EVM smart contract -> tranche database communication there is a precompile smart contract that acts as an API. A smart contract written in Solidity can call this precompile with tightly packed ABI-encoded data, and based on the length of data provided, a certain function is called in the precompile.

| Input Byte Length  | Function               | Expected Input Types                         
|--------------------|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
| 60                 | Unwrap Qi              | to address, amount uint256, etx gas limit uint64                     
| 53                 | Claim Coinbase Lockup  | original miner address, to address, lockup byte uint8, epoch uint32, etx gas limit uint64                    
| 25                 | Get Lockup Data        | original miner address, lockup byte uint8, epoch uint32
| 21                 | Get Latest Lockup Data | original miner address, lockup byte uint8
| 20                 | Claim Qi Deposit       | deposit owner address
| 0                  | Get Latest Epoch       | No input
&nbsp;
 
| Function               | Return Types                                                                        | Explanation             
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------
| Unwrap Qi              | No return                                                                           | Convert wrapped Qi into real Qi (to a Qi address)
| Claim Coinbase Lockup  | No return                                                                           | Claim an unlocked Qi or Quai tranche for its reward including interest
| Get Lockup Data        | trancheUnlockHeight, balance, elements, delegate (32 bytes per element, ABI-encoded)| Get all data for a particular tranche
| Get Latest Lockup Data | trancheUnlockHeight, balance, elements, delegate (32 bytes per element, ABI-encoded)| Get all data for the latest tranche (not yet sealed)
| Claim Qi Deposit       | amount claimed (32 bytes)                                                           | Move a wrapped Qi deposit into a contract's balance
| Get Latest Epoch       | latest epoch (32 bytes, but only up to 4 bytes will be filled)                      | Get the latest global epoch number
&nbsp;
 
**The Lockup Contract lives at address 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000000A**

All of the inputs should be encoded with **abi.encodePacked()** and all of the outputs should be decoded with **abi.decode()**. Here is an example:
```
address public immutable lockupContract = address(0x000000000000000000000000000000000000000A);
function GetLockupForEpoch(address beneficiary, uint8 lockupByte, uint32 epoch) public returns (uint32 trancheUnlockHeight, uint256 balance, uint32 elements, address delegate) {
        (bool success, bytes memory ret) = lockupContract.call(abi.encodePacked(beneficiary, lockupByte, epoch));
        (trancheUnlockHeight, balance, elements, delegate) = abi.decode(ret, (uint32, uint256, uint32, address));
        emit Lockup(beneficiary, trancheUnlockHeight, balance, elements, delegate);
        return (trancheUnlockHeight, balance, elements, delegate);
    }
```
&nbsp;

When a miner wishes to mint a token representing their locked position or tranche, the miner will specify their address, the lockup byte they used, and their epoch. The **quai_getLockupsForContractAndMiner** RPC API call can help the miner provide this data, as this API call expects the contract address and miner address and returns a JSON mapping of epoch to lockup byte to lockup object (with object fields balance, trancheUnlockHeight, elements, delegate). The miner can use a frontend application ("dApp") that parses this data and presents it to the miner in a human-readable format and then call a function on the smart contract with this data. The contract must then verify that the sender owns this tranche via a signature or by checking `msg.sender`. Note that in the case of a Qi tranche, the miner cannot call a function on the smart contract with their Qi address, so they must sign a message with their Qi address and provide it to the contract. 

Once the contract verifies that the caller owns the address, the contract calls the Lockup Contract precompile with these ABI-encoded parameters: original miner address, lockup byte uint8, epoch uint32 and the returned data is the associated ABI-encoded tranche information. The contract then optionally calls the Lockup Contract with no data to get the latest global epoch and ensures that the miner-provided epoch is less than the global epoch. This step is optional because minting an NFT that represents a tranche at the current global epoch doesn't break anything, but this NFT becomes difficult to value, because the miner might mine more coinbases to this tranche before the global epoch increments. In either case, after this step the contract mints an NFT to the miner with the following data attached to it: unique NFT ID, original miner address, balance, unlock height, lockup byte, epoch, and delegate (if not zero-address). The miner may then list this tokenized version of their tranche on a decentralized exchange, or transfer it to another user by changing the owner of the token (but not the original miner address). The NFT ID may be a hash of all of the provided data because it is guaranteed to be unique for a given smart contract. 

When a user wishes to claim an unlocked tranche, the user calls the claim function on the smart contract and provides three parameters: NFT ID uint256, to address, and etxGasLimit uint64. The contract reads the unlock height in the NFT to ensure it has unlocked, then calls the Lockup Contract with the following ABI-encoded data: original miner address, to address, lockup byte uint8, epoch uint32, ETX gas limit uint64. The Lockup Contract precompile checks that the tranche exists and is unlocked (call will revert otherwise), and generates an ETX that sends the tranche amount to the "to" address specified. Note that a Quai lockup can only be claimed with a "to" address in Quai and a Qi lockup can only be claimed with a "to" address in Qi. 

The ETX gas limit is subtracted from the gas supplied to the transaction by the user and is provided to the ETX that is generated. A Qi tranche, which is a tranche that was originally mined by a Qi address, generally requires significanlty more gas than a Quai tranche, because a Qi ETX might create many UTXOs in the Qi ledger for a given number of Qits. For example, 5755 Qits is 5.755 Qi, but requires 6 denominations/UTXOs - a 5000 Qit denomination, a 500 Qit denomination, two 100 Qit denominations, a 50 Qit denomination and a 5 Qit denomination, and each is a separate UTXO that is created in the Qi ledger. Therefore the general recommendation is 500,000 gas for a Qi tranche claim and 100,000 gas for a Quai tranche claim, but these numbers may vary. After the call to the Lockup Contract completes, the smart contract burns the NFT.

It's also possible, although not required, for the smart contract to generate an NFT that represents multiple tranches. As long as the NFT contains all of the necessary data and the unlock period is around the same time for all of the tranches, which is likely if they are the same lockup byte and epoch, the NFT can suitably capture all of the value contained in those tranches. This makes it easier for users to buy large amounts of locked tokens in bulk. For example, a large mining pool might want to aggregate many tranches that are of the same epoch and lockup byte into one NFT, or an investor might want to aggregate many of his own tranche NFTs of the same epoch and lockup byte into one to make it easier to sell. Because each NFT or tranche is valued independently anyways, it doesn't become much more difficult to value an NFT that is a collection of tranches, as long as they all unlock at about the same time (give or take a few days).

### Delegate Address

It is permissible for a miner to include both a smart contract address and a delegate address in their Work Object Header data field. The delegate address does not have control over the tranche from the protocol's perspective - the smart contract still technically owns the tranche. However, the smart contract might allow the delegate address to perform actions on behalf of the miner, so that the miner does not have to manually mint or manage NFTs. The delegate might be a smart contract that calls functions on the owner smart contract to manage tranches on behalf of a miner or mining pool. This implementation is not protocol-defined and is it is up to the owner smart contract to decide if it wants to support delegate addresses.

### Wrapped Qi

The "Unwrap Qi" and "Claim Qi Deposit" functions are for wrapped Qi, a mechanism by which Qi tokens can be represented and used as a QRC20 token in the EVM. This allows users to trade Quai for Qi and vice-versa on decentralized exchanges while still maintaining full custody of their Qi. A user can specify a smart contract in the "Data" field of a Qi tx, and the transaction processing logic will emit an ETX with all outputs that spend to an address in the Quai ledger. When the ETX is executed, it will create a wrapped Qi position that is owned by both the specified smart contract and the Quai address. The user must then "claim" the deposit with an EVM transaction to move their position balance into the smart contract's wrapped Qi balance, and at that point the smart contract can mint the user Wrapped Qi QRC20 tokens corresponding to the value of the deposit. Note that as with the Qi->Quai conversion logic, all outputs in the Qi wrapping transaction must spend to the same address in the Quai ledger.
